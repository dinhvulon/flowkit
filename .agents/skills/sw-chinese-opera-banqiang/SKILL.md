## 0.1 Why Divide by Vocal Music System Rather than Regional Genre

| Dimension | **The Qupai System (曲牌体)** (Yuan Zaju, Chuanqi, Kunqu; Chuanju Gaoqiang) | **The Banqiang System (板腔体)** (Peking, Yu, Yue, Qinqiang, Pingju, Huju, Huaiju; Cantonese Banghuang) |
|---|---|---|
| **Structural Unit** | Zaju: **Four Acts and a Wedge (四折一楔子)**; Chuanqi: Dozens of **Exits (出)** | **Scenes (场 - Chang)** |
| **Lyric Composition** | **Filling Words into Fixed Patterns (填词)**: Pre-existing tune patterns with strict syllable counts, tonal patterns (Ping/Ze), and suites. | **Paired Couplets (上下句)**: 7- or 10-character couplets; metrical variations (板式 - Banshi) drive emotional shifts; open line counts. |
| **Dramaturgical Focus** | Melodic suite structure, modal keys (Gongdiao), poetic metre. | Banshi-to-emotion mapping, rhyming categories (十三辙 - Shisanzhe). |

Regional genre labels (Peking vs. Yu vs. Yue) merely dictate which regional dialect dictionary and melodic variations to reference. **Dramaturgical method is determined by the vocal system, whereas regional genres determine the aesthetic temperament.**

## 0.2 The Opera Medium Boundary

| Dramaturgical Element | Western / General Screenwriting Rule | Traditional Chinese Opera Reality |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Blake Snyder Beat Sheet (15 beats, page anchors). | **Inapplicable**. Opera operates as an episodic, fluid succession of theatrical scenes (场). |
| **Scene Balancing** | Balanced exposition. | **Paichang Alternation Rule (排场交替律)**: Alternates hot (fast, loud, acrobatic) and cold (slow, lyric, intimate) scenes to manage performer stamina and audience attention. |
| **Dual Architecture** | Drama exists on a single narrative plane. | **Technical Architecture (技术结构)**: Narrative plot must intertwine with a technical layout of singing, speech, acting, and acrobatics (唱念做打). |
| **Dramatic Suspense** | Suspense generated by withholding information from the audience. | **Dramatic Irony Preferred**: Secrets are withheld from on-stage characters, rarely from the audience. |
| **Characterization** | Three-dimensional, individualized character arcs. | **Role-Type (行当 - Hangdang) Precedes Individuality**: Character psychology is mediated through established stylistic archetypes. |
| **Dramatic Conflict** | Requires an active, physically present antagonist. | **Suppressed / Structural Conflict**: Permitted; tragic circumstances often stem from institutional fate ("misfortune without a villain"). |
| **Stage Directions** | Purely visual, literary prose; no technical jargon. | **Stylized Physical Conventions (科介 / 程式 - Chengshi)**: Technical stage choreography is an integral part of the primary dramatic text. |
| **Lyric vs. Melody** | Playwrights ignore musical arrangements. | **Playwright governs metric phrasing and Ping/Ze tonal closures**; banshi notations serve as formal musical proposals. |
| **Production Scale** | Spectacle requires complex physical sets. | **The "One Table and Two Chairs" Axiom (一桌二椅)**: Opera is virtual and symbolic; physical sets must never encumber the performer's body. |

## I. System & Genre Typology

### 1. The Four-Part Classification (Fan Junhong)
1. **Singing-Focused Plays (唱工戏)**: Protagonists trapped in deep moral or emotional dilemmas; text is dominated by extended lyrical suites. Speech and movement exist purely to pivot between musical blocks.
2. **Acting-Focused Plays (做工戏)**: Dynamic, character-driven plots with pronounced physical business and spoken repartee (e.g., Flower Dan / Chou comedies).
3. **Acrobatic-Focused Plays (武戏)**: Physical conflict, battle formations, and rhythmic martial arts (Wusheng / Wuchou).
4. **Integrated Masterworks (唱做并重戏)**: Major dramatic vehicles requiring balanced virtuosity across all disciplines.

*Rule of Thumb*: Wusheng acrobatic leads should sing no more than 6 to 8 lines at a stretch, leaning toward free-rhythm Sanban or Yaoban metres.

---

## II. Focusing the Central Dramatic Line

- **Vertical Concentration (Fan Junhong)**: Focus on a single central chain of cause and effect. Adding lateral subplots multiplies entrances and exits, fracturing the opera's lyrical flow.
- **Weng Ouhong's Classical Devices**:
  - *Weideng Xiajian (帷灯匣剑 - The Hidden Sword)*: A major scene where the protagonist does not physically appear on stage, yet every spoken line directly drives the protagonist's impending choice.
  - *Hongyun Tuoyue (烘云托月 - Painting Clouds to Reveal the Moon)*: Secondary characters establish the protagonist's legendary stature prior to their physical entrance.
  - *Xuanbing Xushi (选兵蓄势 - Marshaling Troops Before Battle)*: Introduce essential props and relationships early before unleashing the dramatic crisis.

---

## III. Technical Structure & Scene Choreography (排场)

1. **The Alternation of Hot and Cold Scenes (排场交替律)**:
   - If Scene 1 is a quiet lyric reflection (cold), Scene 2 must introduce active social friction or martial energy (hot).
   - If Acts II and III feature prolonged dramatic laments, Act IV must insert comedic business (Chou) or vibrant martial movement.
2. **Managing Performer Stamina**: Never place two demanding, high-register singing suites in consecutive scenes for the same performer. Use intermediary transitional scenes (*Guochang*) to allow vocal recovery and costume changes.

---

## IV. Role-Types (行当 - Hangdang) & Star Vehicles

Characters are designed through the vocabulary of established archetypes:
- **Two Vocal Architectures**:
  - *Large Voice (大嗓 - Da Sang)*: Chest resonance. Governs Old Sheng (老生), Painted Face (花脸 / 净), Old Dan (老旦), and Clown (丑).
  - *Small Voice (小嗓 - Xiao Sang)*: Falsetto resonance. Governs Dan (旦) and Young Sheng (小生).
- **Metric Taboos**:
  - The Painted Face (Jing) traditionally avoids *Fan-Erhuang* and slow *Erhuang Manban*.
  - *Nanbangzi* is restricted strictly to Dan and Young Sheng.
  - *Suona Erhuang* is reserved for martial heroes and martial deities (Guan Yu).

---

## V. The Dramatic Function of Stylized Conventions (程式 - Chengshi)

- **Write Choreographic Suites, Not Isolated Body Actions**: A playwright indicates recognized movement suites (e.g., *Qiba* martial preparation, *Tangma* horse riding, *Zoubian* night scouting), leaving individual gestural mechanics (sweeping water-sleeves, spinning waist moves) to the performer.
- **The Modern Scene Break Condition**: When an opera adopts modern proscenium scene curtains, classical formal entrances (*Yinzi*, self-introduction poetry) are discarded in favor of *in medias res* atmospheric openings.
- **The Chorus / Off-Stage Voice (歌队)**: In modern adaptations, off-stage choruses replace the traditional opening and closing couplets, serving three specific dramaturgical functions: **anchoring time and space, articulating subconscious interiority, and commenting morally on the action**.

## VI. Lyric Craft (唱词 - Changci)

### 6a. Paired Couplets (上下句) & Phrasing
- **The Metric Phrasing Norm**:
  - *7-Character Line*: Structured as **2 · 2 · 3** (*Example: 提篮子 / 叫卖声 / 走街坊*).
  - *10-Character Line*: Structured as **3 · 3 · 4** (*Example: 见娇儿 / 泪汪汪 / 肝肠寸断*).
- **Tonal Architecture (Ping/Ze)**:
  - *Upper Line (上句 - Shangju)*: Concludes on an **Oblique Tone (仄声 - Ze)**; unrhymed.
  - *Lower Line (下句 - Xiaju)*: Concludes on a **Level Tone (平声 - Ping)**; **strictly rhymed**.
- **Even-Number Law**: An aria suite must consist of an even number of lines. The only legitimate structural exit for an odd-numbered passage is marking a sudden rhythmic sweep (*Saotou - 扫头*).

### 6b. The Pivot Line (重点句)
Every major aria possesses a single, memorable "Pivot Line"—the poetic core encapsulating the character's moral revelation. **The rhyme scheme of the entire aria is selected based on this single line**, and the aria itself is named after it.

### 6c. Lu Jun's Three Virtues of Lyric Writing
1. **Selection of Rhyme (韵选好)**: Broad rhymes (*Ren-Chen, Jiang-Yang, Yan-Qian*) provide abundant vocabulary for fluid storytelling; narrow rhymes (*Ye-Xie, Fa-Hua*) create distinctive, sharp dramatic poignancy.
2. **Structural Braiding (结构编织好)**: Avoid unbroken rows of identical 7- or 10-character lines. Break monotony using asymmetric line insertions, call-and-response questions, and *Duoju* variations.
3. **Thematic Placement (布局好)**: Place extended musical arias strictly at moments of peak emotional, moral, or philosophical crisis. Minor exposition belongs in speech.

### 6d. Global Rhyme Schemes (十三辙 - The Thirteen Rhymes)
- Standard Peking opera lyrics adhere strictly to the **Thirteen Rhyme Categories (十三辙 - Shisanzhe)**.
- In contentious dramatic duets, contrasting characters should hold separate, opposing rhyme categories (*Example: In White Snake's Broken Bridge, Bai Suzhen sings Jiang-Yang while Xu Xian answers in Yan-Qian*).

### 6e. Piled-Up Lines (垛句 - Duoju) & Colloquial Phrasing
- **Duoju Mechanics**: Inserting high-speed clusters of 3-syllable or 4-syllable rhythmic phrases within the lower line (Xiaju) of a metered section to depict mounting rage, excitement, or panic.
- **The Subtraction Test**: If all *Duoju* phrases are removed, the remaining primary couplet must still form a grammatically complete, poetic sentence.

---

## VII. Spoken Dialogue (宾白 - Binbai) & Sung-Spoken Seams

- **The Vocative Interface (称呼语接口 - Universal Banqiang Law)**:
  Spoken dialogue transitions into singing via a direct, punctuated vocative call:
  `"Mother! (Sings) Mother, please take your seat..."`
  *Rule*: The line directly preceding an aria should be an explicit vocative call or an active physical gesture pushing the character into music.
- **Speech Underpins Group Scenes, Singing Underpins Confrontation**: Group scenes rely on spoken banter; intimate two-handers escalate into musical duets.
- **Cutting Speech Mid-Metre**: In free-rhythm metres (*Sanban, Yaoban*), a character may deliver half a sung line, pause for spoken dialogue, and conclude the musical phrase.

---

## VIII. Duet Choreography (对唱 - Duichang)

In Banqiang opera, duets are constructed through metric and line-length deceleration or acceleration:
- **Gou Si-yao (狗撕咬 - Snapping Dog Repartee)**: Staggered, overlapping couplets accelerating from 4 lines $\to$ 2 lines $\to$ 1 line $\to$ half-lines, used for fierce trials or rapid-fire comedic domestic quarrels.
- **The Decisive Finishing Couplet**: In a climactic duel of wits, **the character commanding moral or dramatic dominance must deliver the final Lower Line (Xiaju)**.

---

## IX. Script Notation: Two Opposing Paradigms

| Feature | The Classical Peking Opera Notation (Weng Ouhong) | The Modern Literary Yue Opera Notation (Luo Huaizhen) |
|---|---|---|
| **Musical Metres** | Explicitly marks all banshi (*Xipi Yuanban, Erhuang Manban*). | Omits banshi names entirely; relies on explicit line lengths (5, 7, 4, 3 syllables) to suggest tempo. |
| **Stage Choreography** | Explicitly logs percussion calls (*[Fengrudong], [Daluo Chongji]*), entrance cues, and prop lists. | Uses modern screenwriting layout: scene headings, `[Subtitles: ...]`, and literary action prose. |
| **Role Types** | Explicitly heads dialogue with *Sheng, Dan, Jing, Chou*. | Uses character names; hangdang archetypes are implied through dramatic function. |

*Playwright's Boundary*: Marking banshi is an **artistic proposal**, not an unbending musical decree. Composers and performers adapt metres to fit vocal registers.

---

## X. Interfacing with Performers and Master Musicians

1. **Text Revisions Mandate Melodic Revisions (改词必改腔)**: Modifying a single character in a locked script changes the vocal contour and instrumental accompaniment.
2. **The "Tonal Conflict" (倒字) Law**: Ensure poetic tones do not clash with vocal melodic contours: *Level tones (Yinping) must never drop into a low register; departing tones must not rise awkwardly.*
3. **Writing for the Master Performer**: Provide expansive metric variation (*Changduanju*) so virtuoso singers have space to improvise melismatic vocal ornamentation.

---

## XI. Adapting Traditional Repertoire: Three Categories

- **Restoration (整理)**: Preserving the authentic dramatic spine while smoothing archaic diction and awkward scene transitions.
- **Adaptation (改编)**: Pruning superstitious or reactionary elements, restructuring acts, and introducing modern psychological clarity.
- **Transplantation (移植)**: Taking a successful play from one regional opera style and rewriting its poetic metres, dialect rhymes, and musical cues to fit a new local genre.

---

## XII. Three Contemporary Development Paths

### 12a. Modern-Theme Opera (现代戏)
- Dramatizing contemporary historical or modern life using stylized opera mechanics.
- **The Transformation Law**: Classical stage conventions (riding crops, water-sleeves, artificial beards) must be translated into rhythmic modern physical equivalents:
  $$\text{Real-Life Everyday Action} \longrightarrow \text{Exaggeration} \longrightarrow \text{Rhythmic Stylization} \longrightarrow \text{Choreographed Movement}$$
- *The Percussion Test*: Every major physical action in a modern opera must be capable of landing on an exact percussion beat.

### 12b. Absurdist & Cross-Temporal Reinterpretation (*Pan Jinlian*)
Using historical archetypes to deconstruct modern psychological hypocrisies (e.g., introducing contemporary literary figures as a "chorus of commentators").

### 12c. Contemporary Classical Reinterpretation (*Luo Huaizhen*)
- *"Modernity is purification."* Reducing scenic clutter to symbolic minimalist elements.
- **The Recoding Protocol**: Retain the traditional prop/costume $\to$ alter its physical scale or material $\to$ invert its thematic meaning.

## XIII. Definitive Masterwork Case Study: *The Pouch of Red Gauze* (锁麟囊)

Weng Ouhong's masterpiece written for Cheng Yanqiu illustrates the gold standard of Banqiang engineering:
1. **Modal Architecture**: Vocal modes (*Xipi* vs. *Erhuang*) designate distinct social worlds and long structural chapters rather than fleeting momentary emotions.
2. **Symmetry of Rare Metres**: The flowing *Er-liu (二六)* metre appears only twice in the entire work, positioned with perfect structural symmetry across the wedding and the flood revelation.
3. **Metric Economy**: 40% of the text is set to free-rhythm *Sanban/Yaoban*; fully metered *Shangban* arias are concentrated exclusively in four decisive crisis scenes.
4. **Global Rhyme Progression**: Every scene shifts to a new rhyme category (*Yunzhe*), with the opening and finale bookended by the expansive *Yao-Tiao* rhyme.

## XIV. Master Diagnostic Checklist

- [ ] Is the primary genre established (Singing-focused, Acting-focused, Acrobatic, or Integrated)?
- [ ] Are technical technical requirements (singing blocks vs acrobatic suites) balanced across scenes?
- [ ] Do hot and cold scenes alternate to protect the lead performer's stamina?
- [ ] Are role-type (Hangdang) metric and vocal taboos strictly respected?
- [ ] Does every aria suite contain an **even number of lines** (or conclude on an explicit *Saotou*)?
- [ ] Can all *Duoju* phrases be deleted while leaving the remaining couplet grammatically sound?
- [ ] Have all lower lines (Xiaju) been checked against the [Thirteen Rhymes Table](reference-shisanzhe.md)?
- [ ] Is there an explicit vocative interface (`"Mother! (Sings)"`) or clear physical cue directly preceding every aria?
- [ ] In rapid-fire duets, does the dominant character claim the final lower rhyming line?
- [ ] Does the piece hold up under the minimalist "One Table and Two Chairs" staging constraint?

## XV. Step-by-Step Banqiang Playwriting Workflow

```
[1. Vocal System & Genre] ➔ [2. The Central Conflict Spine] ➔ [3. Technical Scene Matrix (Cold/Hot)]
         ➔ [4. Identify Pivot Lines (重点句)] ➔ [5. Rhyme & Metre Allocation]
         ➔ [6. Drafting Couplets & Vocative Seams] ➔ [7. Metric Pacing & Duoju]
         ➔ [8. Musical & Performer Rehearsal Reading]
```

1. **Step 1: System Selection**: Confirm vocal music system (Banqiang) and role-type casting.
2. **Step 2: Narrative Spine**: Define the central crisis, moral dilemma, and core character arcs.
3. **Step 3: Technical Layout (排场)**: Map scenes across alternating hot and cold energies.
4. **Step 4: The Pivot Lines**: Compose the 2–3 brilliant, defining lyrical couplets that anchor the major scenes.
5. **Step 5: Metric Architecture**: Assign banshi metres (*Manban, Yuanban, Kuaiban, Sanban*) and rhyme categories.
6. **Step 6: Lyric Drafting**: Draft paired couplets adhering to 2-2-3 or 3-3-4 phrasing, inserting vocative interfaces before arias.
7. **Step 7: Rhythmic Acceleration**: Integrate *Duoju* and rapid-fire duets (*Gou Si-yao*) at moments of peak conflict.
8. **Step 8: Rehearsal Polish**: Read lyrics aloud with the composer and lead performer to eliminate tonal conflicts (*Daozi*).
