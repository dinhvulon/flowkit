Round houses built from mammoth bones, hearths that burn bone instead of wood, and Ice Age humans who don't share a single word with me. No modern gear, only what they use. Over 24 hours I learn how Ice Age people actually survived the cold: frostnip first aid, why you never eat snow, stone-boiling water in a hide bag, eating fat with every bite of meat, freezer pits dug into frozen ground, fox-fur clothing and eyed bone needles. Then the hunters take me out onto the mammoth steppe, past a woolly rhino and steppe bison, to a woolly mammoth herd. I get too close, the mother mammoth charges, and the only way out is an icy riverbank.

📖 Background:
Mezhyrich is one of the best-preserved Ice Age settlements in Europe: dwellings built from mammoth jaws, tusks and long bones, ringed by pits and work areas. Radiocarbon dating (Leiden University, 2025) places its largest dwelling around 18,000 years ago, in the coldest phase of the last glaciation, and suggests the houses were occupied only for short stays. Some questions are still argued by archaeologists, like whether the huts were homes or storage, whether the bones came from hunted mammoths or natural bone piles, and whether the painted skull was really played as a drum. When the science isn't settled, I say so in the video.

Survival methods shown follow cold-weather first aid and survival guidance (frostnip, wind chill, cold-induced dehydration, keeping feet dry). Archaeology references: Mezhyrich site reports and lithic studies, Sungir burial studies, d'Errico et al. 2018 on eyed bone needles, Mezhyrich amber and fossil-shell trade, and studies of Ice Age wolves and dogs at Eliseevichi.
