## Description

I survived 24 hours in an Ice Age mammoth-bone camp, 16,000 BC. I'm an archaeologist, and I've dug at this exact site. This time it was alive, and a woolly mammoth charged me.
Subscribe and turn on 🔔 for the next "I Survived 24 Hours" episode.

Mezhyrich, central Ukraine, in the harshest phase of the last Ice Age. Four round houses built from mammoth bones, hearths burning bone instead of wood, and hunter-gatherers who don't share a single word with me. No tent, no gear, only what they use. Over 24 hours I learn how they actually survive: frostnip first aid, why you never eat snow, stone-boiling water in a hide bag, drinking before you're thirsty, eating fat with every bite of meat, freezer pits in frozen ground, fox-fur clothing, and scraping hides with flint. Then the hunters take me to a woolly mammoth herd, I get too close, and a mother mammoth decides I'm a threat.

📖 Background:
Mezhyrich is one of the best-preserved Ice Age settlements in Europe: dwellings built from mammoth jaws, tusks and long bones, surrounded by pits and work areas. New radiocarbon dating (Leiden University, 2025) places its largest dwelling around 18,000 years ago, in the coldest stretch of the last glaciation. Some questions are still argued by archaeologists, like whether these huts were homes or storage, whether the bones came from hunted mammoths or old natural bone piles, and whether the painted skull was really played as a drum. When the science isn't settled, I say so in the video.

Survival methods shown are based on cold-weather first aid and survival guidance (frostnip, wind chill, cold-induced dehydration, keeping feet dry). Archaeology references: Mezhyrich site reports and lithic studies, Sungir burial studies, d'Errico et al. 2018 on eyed bone needles, Mezhyrich amber and fossil-shell trade, and studies of Ice Age wolves and dogs at Eliseevichi.
