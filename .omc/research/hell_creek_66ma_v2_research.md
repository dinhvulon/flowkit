### Derived (our physics, not from a paper. Treat as plausible, not verified)
- **Horizon drop:** the Earth curves away about d²/2R ≈ 3000²/(2×6371) ≈ **700 km** over 3,000 km. The initial fireball and bolide (below ~100 km altitude) are **hidden below the horizon**, so the vlogger would **not** see the impact flash directly. Only the expanding ejecta/vapour plume, if it rose hundreds of km, might show as a glow or dome above the SSE horizon. **Showing the crater fireball on camera is not physical.**
- **Air-blast / sound:** at ~0.3 km/s, a pressure wave would need roughly **2.5–3 hours** to cover 3,000 km. The popular "500–620 mph winds" figures are unsourced and refer to near-field effects. Do not put a hurricane blast in the first minutes at Hell Creek.
- **Time of day is unknown** (no source fixes it). Chicxulub and Hell Creek are at similar longitudes, so local solar time was similar at both. The script may choose a time freely.
- **Day length:** a Late Cretaceous day was **~23.5 h** (372 days/year; de Winter 2020). This is a fun fact only and does not change the lighting. [AGU](https://news.agu.org/press-release/ancient-shell-shows-days-were-half-hour-shorter-70-million-years-ago/), [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/paleontology/cretaceous-earth-day-08211.html)

## 5. Realism and lighting vocabulary (documentary practice and Veo guidance)

| Finding | Use | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Wildlife DP Paul Atkins ASC prefers **back and side light**: it outlines the animal and separates foreground, middle ground and background. Front light looks flat. Shooting a whole sequence in backlight hides time-of-day changes between shots, which helps continuity across clips. | Default to back/side light for animals. ## Natural light phrase bank (Hell Creek, ~55°N, boreal spring)
Use one block per time-of-day and copy it unchanged into every clip of that segment. None of these use god rays, volumetric beams, studio rim light, or the words "cinematic lighting" / "volumetric".

| Time (story clock) | Phrase |
|---|---|
| **Pre-dawn / dawn** (T-12h if impact ~midday+) | `cool blue pre-dawn twilight turning to soft pale-gold light on the eastern horizon, low river mist lying over still brown water, damp air, muted colours, dew on leaves, long soft shadows` |
| **Early morning** (sun 5–20°) | `low raking early-morning sunlight from behind the subject, warm side light skimming the grey-brown mud and catching the edges of leaves and skin, thin haze over the river, deep soft shadows, natural documentary exposure` |
| **Mid-morning** | `clear humid spring morning, sun at a moderate angle, soft broken cloud, natural dappled shade under small broadleaf trees, crisp detail, true-to-life muted greens and browns` |
| **Noon** (sun ~50°, never overhead) | `bright overcast midday, high thin cloud diffusing the sun, even soft light with gentle shadows under bodies, humid haze softening distant treelines, natural colour` (prefer this to hard sun) |
| **Afternoon** | `warm afternoon side light through scattered cumulus, moving cloud shadows sliding across the floodplain, light haze, natural contrast` |
| **Pre-impact (calm)** | `still, warm, slightly hazy spring afternoon, soft diffuse sunlight, birds and insects active, ordinary peaceful light` (deceptive calm, Rule 50 / fk-scriptwriter) |
| **T+6–13 min (quake)** | Same light as the previous clip. Only the motion changes: `trees sway with no wind, water sloshing, dust shaken off branches`. Do not change the sky. |

## Debated / uncertain
1. **Tanis interpretation.** It is widely reported and the timing is consistent, but some scientists remain cautious. DePalma et al. 2021 (season) carries an editor's note about data reliability. A University of Manchester investigation (Dec 2023) found **poor research practice amounting to misconduct but no fabrication**, and the spring conclusion stands (it was found independently by During et al. 2022). [Manchester](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/palaeontologist-cleared-of-fabricating-data-in-dino-killing-asteroid-paper/), [Wikipedia Tanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanis_(fossil_site))
2. **Spherule timing.** The EarthDate summary of the PNAS paper gives **13–25 min**. Sci.News and Uppsala give "**within 45 min – 1 h**". Use 15–60 min.
3. **Thermal pulse strength.** Robertson et al. 2004/2013 describe hours of intense IR (an oven "on broil", ~10 kW/m²) able to ignite dry litter. **Goldin & Melosh 2009** found that settling spherules **self-shield**, giving a peak of 5–15 kW/m², only ~30 min above normal sunlight and only a few minutes above 5 kW/m². Later work (Johnson et al.) argues fine dust could restore a stronger pulse. **Belcher et al.** found **less charcoal than background** at 8 North American K-Pg sites, which argues against continent-wide wildfire. → **For video:** show heat, a glowing sky, scorched leaves and a few local smouldering fires in dry litter, not a forest inferno. [fossilforum excerpt of Goldin & Melosh](https://www.thefossilforum.com/topic/10990-nat-geo-incinerator-earth/), [phys.org Robertson 2013](https://m.phys.org/news/2013-03-global-asteroid-impact-k-pg-extinction.html), [Royal Holloway](https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/items/d50cf604-1b35-8641-e81d-23726ce2ac05/1), [Univ. Lille talk](https://webtv.univ-lille.fr/video/1827/assessing-the-evidence-for-extensive-wildfires-at-the-cretaceous-tertiary-boundary)
4. **Burned wood at Tanis.** Burned trunks and logs are reported (Sci.News), but the cause and timing are not explained.
5. **Sky colour after impact.** "Red-hot sky for hours" is a press quote (Robertson). The bright meteor-streak sky during ejecta re-entry follows from the re-entry models but has no published visual description. The colour of the later dust/soot sky is our inference (dim, brown-grey). How long the darkness lasted (months to >10 years) is debated.
6. **Paleolatitude.** Only one model was checked (PBDB GPlates, ~58°N).
7. **The Thescelosaurus leg** dying on impact day is unpublished and disputed. Its scaly skin is solid.
8. **Dakotaraptor** classification is disputed. **Acheroraptor** may be a juvenile or morph of it.
9. **Brachychampsa size and diet** come from secondary sources. The **Borealosuchus** size is for the Paleocene species.
10. **Hell Creek warm spell just before K-Pg** (Wilf et al. 2003) was not verified in this session.
11. **Pachycephalosaurus** size estimates range from 4.5 m (usual) to 8 m (AMNH outlier).
