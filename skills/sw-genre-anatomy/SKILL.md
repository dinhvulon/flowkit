# Genre Anatomy & The Beat Systems (sw-genre-anatomy)

> **Output Language:** English (or matching user query language). All terminology anchors to original craft terms (see [terms.md](../sw-workflow/terms.md)).

Synthesized from John Truby’s masterwork *The Anatomy of Genres: How Story Forms Explain the Way the World Works* (2022). It forms a complementary pair with Truby's `sw-truby-anatomy`: while that skill governs the universal organic skeleton (the Seven Key Steps and 22 Building Blocks), this skill governs the **Genre Layer**—the specific plot beats, character archetypes, opposing forces, and philosophical worldviews demanded by each genre family.

---

## 1. What is Genre?

### The Three Rules of Play
1. **Rule 1: Genre is the Fundamental Unit of Story Commerce and a System of Beats.**
   - Every major genre is defined by 15 to 25 specialized plot beats: *"These plot beats have more to do with the success of a story than any other element by far."*
   - If you skip a core genre beat (e.g., omitting the First Dance in a Love story), the core audience will feel cheated.
   - Surface tropes (costumes, gadgets, iconic catchphrases) are merely sugar sprinkled on top; the sequence of genre beats is the cake itself.
   - *Audit Test: Strip away all cosmetic genre tropes. Does the dramatic sequence of beats still stand up?*
2. **Rule 2: Modern Popular Storytelling Mixes Three to Four Genres.**
   - The watershed moment was *Star Wars*: combining Fantasy + Science Fiction + Western + Myth + Samurai/Action beats in rapid-fire succession.
   - Two prerequisites:
     - You must master the full beat list of every genre you weave into your story, even the beats you choose to subvert.
     - You must master the specific storytelling techniques of each genre.
     - Mixing typically incompatible genres produces groundbreaking originality (e.g., Sci-Fi + Caper = *Inception*; Gangster + Myth + Fairy Tale = *The Godfather*).
3. **Rule 3: You Must Transcend the Primary Genre.**
   - Hitting every beat is necessary, but not sufficient. To create lasting art, you must transcend genre through three paths:
     - *Twist the Beats:* Reorder, alter the execution, or invert expectations—never merely delete them.
     - *Dramatize the Genre's Underlying Life Philosophy:* Connect your controlling idea directly to the genre's moral core.
     - *Explore the Genre's Unique "Story Form of Life":* Dig into the fundamental existential challenge the genre was invented to explain.

### The Three Genre Families
- **The Myth Family:** Myth, Action, Western.
- **The Crime Family:** Detective, Crime, Thriller, Gangster.
- **The Speculative Family:** Horror, Science Fiction, Fantasy.
*(Mixing within the same family is comfortable; cross-family combinations yield radical originality).*

## 2. The Ladder of Twelve Genres

Truby ranks genres along an ascending developmental ladder based on the central human flaw the protagonist must overcome and the life philosophy explored:

| Genre | Fundamental Concern | Underlying Life Philosophy | Protagonist's Core Flaw | Opponent Archetype | Plot Geometry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Horror** | Religion | Confront death and face your ghosts from the past. | Enslaved mind: binary thinking, fundamental terror, guilt | The Monster (Hero’s dark double) | Linear (closing box) |
| **Action** | Success | 90 percent of success is taking decisive action. | Violence in a shame culture; lone-wolf isolation | The Nemesis (Second-best fighter) | Linear (rapid velocity) |
| **Myth** | The Life Process | Seek immortality by discovering your true destiny. | Orphaned / spiritually lost; unconscious need to become a hero | Successive Strangers | Meander (odyssey) |
| **Memoir / Coming-of-Age** | Creating the Self | Examine your past to create your authentic self. | Bad faith; living to please others; unresolved family wound | Family members / clan | Storyteller frame |
| **Science Fiction** | Science & Culture | Make the right choices now to ensure a better future for all. | Unevolved; crippled understanding of what it means to be human | Authorities / The System | Branching (vortex) |
| **Crime** | Morality & Justice | Protect the weak and bring the guilty to justice. | Cop: impoverished, lonely. Criminal: believes self above the law | The Super-Criminal | Linear (cat-and-mouse) |
| **Comedy** | Manners & Morals | Success comes when you strip away facades and show who you are. | Comic gap: delusions of grandeur; pretentious bumbler | The Pretentious Authority | Linear / Chaos |
| **Western** | Rise & Fall of Society | When you help others build a home, you create a free civilization. | Extreme individualist loneliness; code of shame | The Bad Cowboy (Moral mirror) | Linear / Showdown |
| **Gangster** | Corruption of Power | Do not be enslaved by power and wealth, or pay the ultimate price. | Ruthless ambition; compartmentalized moral code | Six-Corner Opposition | Rise and Fall |
| **Fantasy** | The Art of Living | Discover the magic within yourself that makes life an art form. | Repressed, blinded, incapable of wonder | Authoritarian Parent / Gatekeeper | Meander / Double arena |
| **Detective / Thriller** | Mind & Truth | Uncover the truth and assign guilt in spite of mortal peril. | Limited perspective; starting in absolute ignorance | The Killer among suspects | Spiral / Chinese boxes |
| **Love** | The Art of Happiness | Learning how to love unconditionally is the key to happiness. | Incapable of vulnerability; terrified of surrender | The Lover (Mirror obstacle) | Linear / Double reversal |

## 3. Horror: Religion and the Confrontation with Death

- **Core Engine:** A unique monster relentlessly pursues a victim into an ever-shrinking physical and psychological enclosure.
- **Fundamental Question:** *What is genuinely human, and what is inhuman?*
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Protagonist:* The Victim. Must begin as intensely human with relatable vulnerability.
  - *Opponent:* The Monster. The "Other-in-the-Extreme" and the physical externalization of the protagonist’s repressed guilt.
  - *Ally:* The Rational Skeptic who gives voice to the audience's real-world doubts.
  - *World:* The haunted labyrinth or claustrophobic community—the flawed psyche manifested physically.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **The Ghost is Beat 1:** A horrific crime from the past must be intimately connected to the protagonist's psychological flaw and the monster's current rampage.
  2. **The Monster is the Hero's Double:** The monster must incarnate the hero’s deepest unconscious fear or illicit desire. Without this double, horror devolves into cheap jump scares.
  3. **The Law of Escalating Cost:** Escaping death in each progressive beat demands a steeper physical or psychological price.
  4. **The Pressure Cooker:** Tension must rise monotonically: confined spaces, severing communication lines, physical wounds, and escalating deception.
  5. **Cyclone Reveal Sequence:** Revelations arrive as an accelerating cascade of nightmares converging directly into the final battle.
- **Transcending Horror:** Invert the relationship between hero and monster (*"We are the monsters"*), expanding the horror into societal, political, or cosmic critique.

## 4. Action: The Will to Action and the Philosophy of Success

- **Core Engine:** Life is about achievement under extreme physical pressure: *"You win or you die."*
- **Fundamental Question:** *Fight or flee?*
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Protagonist:* The Fighter driven by an unyielding will to greatness. Their primary weakness stems directly from their strength: they fight best alone and cannot integrate into community.
  - *Opponent:* The Nemesis. The second-best warrior in the world, specifically equipped to exploit the hero's internal flaw.
  - *World:* A threatened community under siege. Arenas alternate between enclosures (fortresses/pressure cookers) and chases (speed/movement).
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **The Opening Definitive Action:** Define the protagonist's physical mastery, tactics, and ethics through an opening crisis before the primary plot ignites.
  2. **Four-Corner Opposition:** Prevent repetitive fisticuffs by deploying a hierarchy of opponents attacking from multiple vectors with contrasting fighting philosophies.
  3. **The Tactical Improvisation Beat:** Mid-story, the hero's meticulous plan must shatter, forcing an instant, ingenious improvisation under fire.
  4. **The Showdown Climax:** Must take place in a tight, hazardous arena, narrowing from multi-party chaos into an intense, one-on-one duel where intellect outplays raw muscle.
  5. **Resolution:** Must decisively answer whether the lone fighter joins the community or rides away into isolation.
- **Transcending Action:** *Show the staggering human cost of victory.* Expand into an Action Epic or Anti-War tragedy where the adversary possesses legitimate moral grievances.

## 5. Myth: The Life Process and the Search for Destiny

- **Core Engine:** Life is an expansive journey to discover who you were born to be: *"Destiny is life's version of immortality."*
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Protagonist:* The Searcher. Driven by an unconscious, spiritual need to become a hero rather than a superficial worldly want.
  - *Opponent:* Successive Strangers encountered along the road, each testing a distinct facet of the hero's soul.
  - *World:* An expansive mythic landscape juxtaposing contrasting cultural systems.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **Seven Steps of Mythic World-Building:** Establish arena → define resources/technology → **lock the moral value opposition of the culture** → map sub-worlds → build social hierarchy → define magic/spiritual rules → project the destination.
  2. **The Talisman:** Must possess hidden power unknown even to the bearer, serving as the material embodiment of the hero's true identity.
  3. **Internalized Geography:** Every stop on the physical journey must mirror a specific psychological death and rebirth.
  4. **Public & Cosmic Epiphany:** The climax must deliver both a public recognition of identity and a profound moral vision for how society should live in harmony.
- **Transcending Myth:** Create a *Creation Myth* exploring the birth of human consciousness, or a *Female Myth* focused on communion, integration, and healing rather than division and conquest.

## 6. Memoir & Coming-of-Age: The Construction of the Self

- **Core Engine:** The individual's journey to create an authentic identity from the wreckage of family trauma and societal indoctrination.
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Protagonist:* Begins in *bad faith*—blaming others, living out parental expectations, or hiding behind cynical masks.
  - *Opponent:* Family members or intimate peers bound by an invisible web of collective guilt and unspoken rules.
  - *Structure:* The **Storyteller Frame**—a present-tense catalyst triggers an excavation of the past that irreversibly alters the present.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **Double Desire Line:** The present-day narrator seeks profound emotional meaning; the past protagonist pursues a singular, urgent concrete goal.
  2. **Rigorous Moral Argument:** The protagonist cannot be an innocent saint; they must acknowledge their own complicity, cowardice, and destructive choices.
  3. **Dual Epiphanies:** The climax must deliver an epiphany for the young protagonist in the past, followed by a second, deeper epiphany for the mature narrator writing in the present.
- **Transcending the Genre:** Interweave personal coming-of-age with broader historical upheaval, creating a fractal mirror between personal growth and societal awakening.

## 7. Science Fiction: Science, Society, and the Future

- **Core Engine:** Examining how human choices today dictate the survival or dystopian collapse of future civilization.
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Protagonist:* The Unevolved Human. Their personal blind spot mirrors the moral bankruptcy or evolutionary stagnation of the planet.
  - *Opponent:* The System / The Authoritarian Machine.
  - *Structure:* Branching narrative converging into a centralized **Vortex Point**.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **Define the Evolutionary Stage of Society:** Wilderness (shame culture) → Village/Town (guilt culture) → Industrial City (consumer culture) → Cyber/Technocratic Oppression (fear culture).
  2. **The Illusion of Freedom:** An authentic dystopia is never maintained solely by brute force; it maintains control through manufactured consent, seductive consumer choices, and bureaucratic deception.
  3. **Fatal Rules of the World:** Establish the physical and social rules of this speculative universe early, attaching lethal consequences to their violation.
  4. **The Vortex Climax:** Cross-cut accelerating storylines toward a single climactic event that will determine the species' future.
- **Transcending Sci-Fi:** Combine hard speculative technology with ecological philosophy, exploring the evolutionary transformation of consciousness.

---

## 8. Crime: Morality, Equality, and Justice

- **Core Engine:** Investigating who is allowed to live above the law in a democratic society.
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Protagonist:* The Master Cop. Underpaid, weary, and personally isolated, yet fiercely devoted to the democratic promise of equal justice.
  - *Opponent:* The Super-Criminal. Highly charismatic and intellectually gifted, genuinely believing their superiority grants them immunity from moral law.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **Crime vs. Detective Distinction:** In a Crime story, the hero’s goal is to **catch the criminal**; in a Detective story, the goal is to **discover who committed the crime**.
  2. **Mutual Deception:** Both cop and criminal are masters of their craft, locked in an escalating duel of feints, misdirection, and tactical traps.
  3. **The Moral Crucible:** Legal justice does not equal moral balance. The climax must demand a brutal personal sacrifice where upholding the law conflicts with true moral decency.
- **Transcending Crime:** Execute an Epic Crime Tragedy where the pursuit of justice exposes the inherent moral rot of the judicial system itself.

## 9. Comedy: Manners, Morals, and the Comic Gap

- **Core Engine:** Exposing human hypocrisy by instantaneously reducing pretentious ego to humble animal reality.
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Protagonist:* The Bumbler with delusions of grandeur.
  - *The Comic Gap:* The vast chasm between who the character pretends to be and who they actually are.
  - *Opponent:* The Pretentious Authority figure who possesses power but lacks true moral authority.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **Story Scenes Precede Gags:** Never string isolated jokes together at the beginning; establish character desires, real stakes, and narrative velocity first.
  2. **Desire Level 10, Competence Level 1:** Give the character an obsession that forces them to do the exact thing they are worst at.
  3. **The Scaffolding of Nightmares:** Anchor the character's greatest irrational terror early, then systematically subject them to escalating variations of that exact humiliation.
- **Transcending Comedy:** Black Comedy (satirizing societal taboos without giving characters comfortable moral redemptions) or biting political Satire.

## 10. Western: The Rise and Fall of Civilization

- **Core Engine:** A lone gunfighter protects settlers building homes out of the wilderness, knowing that the very civilization he builds will have no place for a killer like him.
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Dual Protagonists:* The Cowboy (man of violence) and the Settler/Farmer (man of peace).
  - *Opponent:* The Immoral Gunfighter—the dark reflection of what the hero would become without an ethical code.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **The Doomed Man:** The cowboy’s victory is inherently tragic: by eliminating the lawless elements, he makes his own violent existence obsolete.
  2. **The Showdown as Sacred Ritual:** The final gunfight must be a public, formal trial of moral courage with strict rules of engagement.
- **Transcending the Western:** The **Anti-Western**, inverting the myth by revealing that the arriving "civilization" is even more corrupt and predatory than the wilderness it conquered.

## 11. Gangster: The Corruption of Business and Power

- **Core Engine:** The dark underside of the capitalist dream: an ambitious outsider rises to absolute criminal power, only to find himself trapped in a gilded prison of paranoia.
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Protagonist:* The Gangster Boss. Highly disciplined, loving his family, yet possessing a ruthlessly compartmentalized moral code.
  - *Structure:* The classic **Rise and Fall**.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **Six-Corner Opposition:** The boss must battle rivals, law enforcement, corrupt politicians, ambitious lieutenants, family demands, and his own moral decay simultaneously.
  2. **Shifting Alliances:** Dramatic tension is generated by lieutenants switching loyalties under pressure.
  3. **The Pyrrhic Climax:** The protagonist achieves absolute dominion over his criminal empire, but ends utterly alienated, imprisoned, or murdered by his closest confidant.
- **Transcending the Gangster Genre:** The **Gangster Epic / Epic of the Everyman**, using the mob family as a razor-sharp microscope examining systemic corporate and political corruption.

---

## 12. Fantasy: The Art of Living and Wonder

- **Core Engine:** A spiritually repressed hero journeys into an enchanted alternate realm, discovering the inner creative magic required to transform reality.
- **Character & World Template:**
  - *Dual Arenas:* The mundane grey world (A) → the enchanted realm (B) → returning to transform the mundane world (A2).
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **The World Mirrors the Weakness:** The enchanted realm must directly challenge the protagonist's specific psychological inhibition.
  2. **Ironclad Magic Rules:** Define the source, costs, and limits of magical power early; unconstrained magic destroys dramatic tension.
  3. **Internal Trigger for Return:** The hero's decision to return home must be driven by an internal moral epiphany, never merely an external eviction.
- **Transcending Fantasy:** Social Fantasy and Political Allegory, examining competing philosophies of community organization and human freedom.

---

## 13. Detective & Thriller: The Mind, Truth, and Mortal Danger

- **Core Engine:** Navigating a labyrinth of deception to expose the hidden truth behind a crime.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **Plan the Culprit's Crime First:** Map out the murderer's meticulous timeline, alibis, and framing strategy before writing the investigator's first scene.
  2. **Double Clues:** Every major clue must yield at least two plausible, conflicting interpretations.
  3. **The Spiral Reveal:** Revelations must accelerate in tempo and hit progressively closer to the investigator's personal inner circle.
- **Transcending the Genre:** Cosmic Thrillers where investigating an external crime forces the detective to confront their own suppressed moral guilt.

---

## 14. Love: The Art of Happiness and Vulnerability

- **Core Engine:** Two deeply defensive individuals break through their emotional armor to achieve profound mutual transformation.
- **Hard Rules:**
  1. **The Lover is the Primary Antagonist:** Outside rivals and disapproving parents are secondary; the real battle is between the lovers themselves as they resist surrender.
  2. **Oscillating Polarity (Yes/No Rhythm):** Every moment of romantic intimacy must trigger an immediate defensive retreat as pride and fear reassert themselves.
  3. **The Double Reversal:** In the climax, both lovers must actively sacrifice their most cherished selfish defense to prove authentic devotion.
- **Transcending Love:** Moving beyond courtship to explore the endurance of marriage, confronting the existential labor of long-term partnership.

## 15. The Genre-Mixing Master Worksheet

1. **Lock the Primary Genre:** Identify the protagonist's core flaw and match it to the Master Ladder.
2. **Select 1 to 3 Complementary Secondary Genres:** Favor cross-family pairings (e.g., Sci-Fi + Noir Detective, Horror + Love).
3. **Audit Incompatible Beats:** Identify mutually exclusive genre demands (e.g., Horror's bleak cynicism vs. Love's emotional communion) and explicitly decide which genre takes precedence.
4. **Anchor the Underlying Structural Spine:** Use Mythic or Classical 3-Act geometry to anchor the macro-plotline.
5. **Harmonize the Controlling Philosophy:** Ensure the overarching theme expresses the primary genre’s life philosophy, while secondary genres contribute pacing, texture, and set pieces.

## 16. Universal Genre Diagnostic Checklist

1. What is the protagonist's fundamental internal flaw? Does it match the primary genre?
2. Have all mandatory beats of the primary genre been accounted for?
3. Does the antagonist embody the genre's specific oppositional archetype?
4. Is the plot geometry aligned with the genre's natural rhythm (linear, meander, branching, rise-and-fall)?
5. Does the setting physically externalize the protagonist's inner conflict?
6. Is the mid-story driven by the genre's natural engine (pressure, improvisation, deceit, escalating nightmares)?
7. Does the climax deliver the genre's obligatory showdown, sacrifice, or double reversal?
8. Are secondary genre beats serving the story without fracturing thematic clarity?
9. Have generic tropes been twisted into fresh, modern executions?
10. Does the final image leave a resonant, genre-transcending thematic imprint?
